Game of Thrones is an American fantasy drama television series created by David Benioff and D. B. Weiss for HBO. It is an adaptation of A Song of Ice and Fire, the fantasy novel series by George R. R. Martin. The show premiered on April 17, 2011, and concluded on May 19, 2019, with 73 episodes across eight seasons. Set on the fictional continents of Westeros and Essos, it follows several story arcs at once: a war of succession for the Iron Throne among noble families, the exiled last descendant of the deposed Targaryen dynasty plotting her return, and the Night's Watch defending the realm from an ancient threat beyond the Wall. The series won 59 Primetime Emmy Awards, the most by a scripted primetime series, including Outstanding Drama Series four times.

## For Newcomers

Game of Thrones is a story about power and the people who want it. It is set in a world that looks medieval, with knights and castles and dragons, but the politics feel modern. Every family has its own agenda. Every alliance is temporary. The people you root for die. The people you hate sometimes win.

You do not need to know anything about fantasy to follow it. The story is not really about magic, even though magic exists. It is about families. The Starks, the Lannisters, the Targaryens. Each house has its own motto, its own history, its own grudges. You learn them the way you learn about real families: slowly, through their choices.

Start with season one and pay attention to names. The show rewards patience. It does not explain everything at once, and that is part of why it works. If you have seen any medieval drama, you already know the shape. Game of Thrones just makes it bigger, bloodier, and harder to look away from.

## The Story

The series opens with Ned Stark, Lord of Winterfell, being asked by his old friend King Robert Baratheon to serve as Hand of the King. Ned accepts, and quickly discovers that the court in King's Landing is a nest of secrets. Robert dies. Ned is executed. His death sets off a war between the great houses.

Robb Stark fights for northern independence. Stannis and Renly Baratheon each claim the throne. The Lannisters hold King's Landing for young Joffrey. Across the sea, Daenerys Targaryen, the last surviving child of the deposed king, raises an army and three dragons. North of the Wall, Jon Snow joins the Night's Watch and learns that the real threat is not politics. It is the White Walkers, an army of the dead led by the Night King.

The final seasons bring these threads together. Daenerys arrives in Westeros, helps defeat the dead, then turns on the living. Jon Snow kills her. Drogon melts the Iron Throne. Bran Stark is chosen as king, Sansa rules an independent North, and Jon is exiled beyond the Wall. The ending divided audiences. The show is still argued about years later.

Two prequel series followed. House of the Dragon, set 172 years earlier, tells the story of the Targaryen civil war known as the Dance of the Dragons. A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms, set a century before the original show, follows the hedge knight Ser Duncan the Tall and his squire Egg. Both expand the world without trying to replace what came before.
